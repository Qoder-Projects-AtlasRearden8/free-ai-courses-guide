# Learning paths

> A route with a finish line.

[Guide home](README.md) · [Certificate guide](certificates.md) · [90-day plan](90-day-plan.md) · [Full library](full-library.md)

Five routes, same principle: learn a little, make something small, then check your understanding. Start at the level that matches what you can already do—not the level you think you should be.

| # | Path | Level | Time | Weekly effort |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 01 | [Understand AI without the jargon](#understand-ai) | Beginner | 4–6 weeks | 3–4 hours per week |
| 02 | [Python and data, without the bootcamp](#python-and-data) | Beginner to intermediate | 12 weeks–6 months | 5–6 hours per week |
| 03 | [Build with LLMs, agents, and MCP](#build-with-ai) | Intermediate | 12 weeks–4 months | 5–8 hours per week |
| 04 | [Free AI coding and local models](#practical-ai) | Beginner to intermediate | 6–8 weeks | 4–6 hours per week |
| 05 | [Deep learning, from first principles](#deep-learning) | Advanced | 6–9 months | 6–10 hours per week |

Every route uses free-to-learn resources. Some certificates cost money; each path states its certificate reality before you commit. Progress is not a race: start at the level that matches what you can already do.

<a id="understand-ai"></a>

## 01. Understand AI without the jargon

**Beginner · 4–6 weeks · 3–4 hours per week**

> A calm on-ramp for curious adults who want to use AI thoughtfully and explain it clearly.

Build a working vocabulary, practice a repeatable prompt loop, and develop the judgment to notice when AI is helpful, wrong, or inappropriate.

- **Best for:** Nurses, writers, small-business owners, parents, and anyone who keeps hearing AI terms they are too polite to ask about.
- **Prerequisites:** None. No math, no coding, no prerequisite course.
- **Certificates:** One free Google badge and one free Kaggle certificate are available in this route.

### The route

1. **Get the big picture.** Start with a 45-minute explanation and a friendly university course. The goal is not to memorize terms; it is to replace vague anxiety with a few accurate sentences.
  - [Introduction to Generative AI](https://www.skills.google/course_templates/536) — Google; 45 min; Beginner · Free certificate.
    A 45-minute, no-code explanation of what generative AI is, how it differs from traditional machine learning, and where Google uses it. Finish with a short quiz and a free Google skill badge.
  - [Elements of AI: Introduction to AI](https://www.elementsofai.com/) — University of Helsinki; ~30 hours; Beginner · Paid certificate.
    A gentle, non-technical course from the University of Helsinki and MinnaLearn. Learn what AI can and cannot do, where it shows up in daily life, and how it affects society. No math or coding required.
2. **Learn how machines learn.** Skim Google's ML fundamentals and watch one visual explanation. You are building a mental model of training, prediction, and error—not becoming an engineer in a weekend.
  - [Machine Learning Crash Course](https://developers.google.com/machine-learning/crash-course) — Google; ~15 hours; Beginner.
    Google's interactive tour of machine learning fundamentals: regression, classification, neural networks, large language models, and fairness. Videos, readings, and hands-on exercises make it a strong free foundation.
  - [3Blue1Brown](https://www.youtube.com/@3blue1brown) — 3Blue1Brown; Watch 2–3 videos; All levels.
    Visual, carefully paced explanations of neural networks, calculus, and linear algebra. Start with the neural-network series when jargon starts to blur; the animations build intuition faster than definitions alone.
3. **Practice a real prompt.** Take a short prompting course, then use Google's whitepaper to understand why one prompt works better than another. Test everything on a low-stakes, non-sensitive task.
  - [ChatGPT Prompt Engineering for Developers](https://www.deeplearning.ai/short-courses/chatgpt-prompt-engineering-for-developers/) — DeepLearning.AI; 1 hr 40 min; Beginner · Paid certificate.
    A 1 hour 40 minute course on writing clear instructions, summarizing, inferring, transforming, and expanding text with an LLM API. Basic Python is helpful, but the prompting principles apply far beyond code.
  - [Prompt Engineering (Google Whitepaper)](https://www.kaggle.com/whitepaper-prompt-engineering) — Google; ~1 hour; Intermediate.
    Google's free whitepaper on prompt engineering techniques, model settings, and real examples. It goes deeper than a list of magic phrases and gives you a vocabulary for why a prompt works.
4. **Build your judgment.** Use an ethics course and a human-centered design guide to make your own rules for privacy, verification, disclosure, and when to stop and ask a person.
  - [Kaggle: Intro to AI Ethics](https://www.kaggle.com/learn/intro-to-ai-ethics) — Kaggle Learn; 4 hours; Beginner · Free certificate.
    Four hours on human-centered design, fairness, and privacy for AI systems. The examples are practical, the language is plain, and you leave with a free certificate plus a better checklist for building responsibly.
  - [People + AI Guidebook](https://pair.withgoogle.com/guidebook) — Google; 3–4 hours; Intermediate.
    Google's free, human-centered design guide for AI products. Work through chapters on mental models, explainability, feedback, errors, and trust—especially useful if you want to build tools people actually understand.

### The finish line: A one-page AI field note

Write a plain-English explanation of generative AI in five paragraphs, include a prompt template you tested, and finish with five personal rules for safe use.

**Proof of work:** A document you could hand to a colleague. Bonus: record a five-minute voice note explaining it without jargon.

**Then what?** Move to the Python and data foundations route if you want to make tools yourself, or stay here and deepen your practice with the 30/60/90-day plan.

## 02. Python and data, without the bootcamp

**Beginner to intermediate · 12 weeks–6 months · 5–6 hours per week**

> A practical foundation for people who want to clean data, train a first model, and know when the result is believable.

Learn just enough Python to be dangerous, get comfortable with tabular data, build a first model, and tell an honest story about what it can and cannot prove.

- **Best for:** Anyone willing to practice a little code each week. Useful for authors tracking sales, small businesses studying demand, or healthcare workers learning with public data.
- **Prerequisites:** Comfort using a browser and editing a spreadsheet. No prior programming required, but consistency is.
- **Certificates:** Kaggle certificates are free and quick; freeCodeCamp certifications are free but long.

### The route

1. **Python on-ramp.** Use Kaggle's short in-browser course as the required spine. Keep freeCodeCamp's larger certification in reserve for when you want a long, project-based credential.
  - [Kaggle: Python](https://www.kaggle.com/learn/python) — Kaggle Learn; 5 hours; Beginner · Free certificate.
    Five hours of hands-on Python in the browser: variables, functions, loops, lists, dictionaries, and importing libraries. Short coding exercises and a free completion certificate make it a low-friction first coding step.
  - [Scientific Computing with Python](https://www.freecodecamp.org/learn/scientific-computing-with-python) — freeCodeCamp; ~300 hours (self-paced); Beginner · Free certificate.
    freeCodeCamp's project-heavy Python certification covers variables, loops, functions, objects, and algorithms, ending with five build projects and a free certification. The curriculum is large; do the first sections for a taste.
2. **Make data less scary.** Learn pandas through small exercises, then use freeCodeCamp's data-analysis curriculum for deeper project practice. Pick a public dataset you actually find interesting.
  - [Kaggle: Pandas](https://www.kaggle.com/learn/pandas) — Kaggle Learn; 4 hours; Beginner · Free certificate.
    Four hours of practical data wrangling with pandas: selecting, filtering, grouping, joining, and cleaning real tabular data. Finish with a free certificate and the skills to inspect a dataset without panic.
  - [Data Analysis with Python](https://www.freecodecamp.org/learn/data-analysis-with-python) — freeCodeCamp; ~300 hours (self-paced); Beginner · Free certificate.
    Learn NumPy, pandas, Matplotlib, and Seaborn through lessons and five data-analysis projects, with a free certification at the end. A practical, long-form path for people who like to learn by building.
3. **Train a first model.** Build a decision tree and random forest in Kaggle, then use Google's crash course to understand what the model is optimizing and where it can fail.
  - [Kaggle: Intro to Machine Learning](https://www.kaggle.com/learn/intro-to-machine-learning) — Kaggle Learn; 3 hours; Beginner · Free certificate.
    A three-hour first pass at machine learning with scikit-learn. Build a decision tree and random forest, use a train/test split, and learn why validation matters. No prior ML needed.
  - [Machine Learning Crash Course](https://developers.google.com/machine-learning/crash-course) — Google; ~15 hours; Beginner.
    Google's interactive tour of machine learning fundamentals: regression, classification, neural networks, large language models, and fairness. Videos, readings, and hands-on exercises make it a strong free foundation.
4. **Get honest about evaluation.** Learn about validation, missing data, and pipelines. Read Machine Learning Yearning for the project-level judgment that code tutorials often skip.
  - [Kaggle: Intermediate Machine Learning](https://www.kaggle.com/learn/intermediate-machine-learning) — Kaggle Learn; 4 hours; Intermediate · Free certificate.
    Move beyond a first model with missing-value handling, categorical variables, pipelines, cross-validation, and gradient boosting. Good next practice after Intro to Machine Learning.
  - [Machine Learning Yearning](https://info.deeplearning.ai/machine-learning-yearning-book) — DeepLearning.AI; ~3 hours; Beginner.
    Andrew Ng's free ebook on shaping machine-learning projects: choosing metrics, prioritizing errors, and deciding what to improve next. Short chapters, little math, and unusually practical for beginners and managers.
5. **Make a portfolio project.** Only after the smaller pieces feel familiar, choose a long freeCodeCamp certification or go deeper into neural networks with Kaggle. Use the other as optional follow-up.
  - [Machine Learning with Python](https://www.freecodecamp.org/learn/machine-learning-with-python) — freeCodeCamp; ~300 hours (self-paced); Intermediate · Free certificate.
    A long-form, project-based TensorFlow and Keras certification covering neural networks, classification, clustering, and recommender systems. Five projects lead to a free certificate; it is a marathon, not a weekend.
  - [Kaggle: Intro to Deep Learning](https://www.kaggle.com/learn/intro-to-deep-learning) — Kaggle Learn; 4 hours; Intermediate · Free certificate.
    Use TensorFlow and Keras to build a first neural network, add layers, train with stochastic gradient descent, and recognize overfitting. Four focused hours with a free certificate attached.

### The finish line: One honest data story

Choose a public, non-sensitive dataset. Ask one clear question, establish a baseline, train a simple model, and report one meaningful limit alongside your result.

**Proof of work:** A clean notebook and a one-page README with the question, data source, method, metric, and 'what this does not prove'.

**Then what?** You are ready for the builder route: LLM applications, agents, and MCP. Or continue into deep learning for a more mathematical route.

## 03. Build with LLMs, agents, and MCP

**Intermediate · 12 weeks–4 months · 5–8 hours per week**

> From a first API call to a small agent that uses tools—with free certificates available along the way.

Learn how LLM applications are put together, how agents use tools, and how MCP connects models to outside data. Each stage ends with something small you can run.

- **Best for:** People who can already write a little Python and want to build useful, local-first tools rather than only chat with a model.
- **Prerequisites:** Basic Python (variables, functions, lists, a little file handling), comfort with the command line, and patience with error messages.
- **Certificates:** Hugging Face offers free Agents and MCP certificates. DeepLearning.AI lessons are free; its certificates require PRO.

### The route

1. **Python and API confidence.** Fill the coding gaps with DeepLearning.AI's beginner Python course. Skim Microsoft's Generative AI curriculum for the app-building vocabulary, not as a race.
  - [AI Python for Beginners](https://www.deeplearning.ai/short-courses/ai-python-for-beginners/) — DeepLearning.AI; 11.5 hours; Beginner · Paid certificate.
    A friendly 11.5-hour introduction to Python with an AI helper. Cover variables, functions, data, and small projects; no coding background required. The lessons are free, and the pace is yours.
  - [Generative AI for Beginners](https://github.com/microsoft/generative-ai-for-beginners) — Microsoft; 21 lessons (self-paced); Beginner.
    Twenty-one lessons that move from prompt basics to retrieval-augmented generation, agents, and responsible use, with Python code samples. You can read the ideas without using Azure, though a little coding helps.
2. **Understand the model layer.** Work through the first chapters of the Hugging Face LLM course. Focus on tokens, transformers, datasets, and evaluation before trying to fine-tune anything.
  - [Hugging Face LLM Course](https://huggingface.co/learn/llm-course) — Hugging Face; 6–8 hours per chapter; Intermediate.
    A free, twelve-chapter course on transformers, datasets, tokenizers, fine-tuning, and modern LLM techniques using the Hugging Face ecosystem. It expects good Python and benefits from an intro deep-learning course.
3. **Prompt, call, and constrain.** Use DeepLearning.AI's prompting short course to turn a fuzzy idea into instructions, then enforce a format in code. Keep API keys in environment variables, never in your repo.
  - [ChatGPT Prompt Engineering for Developers](https://www.deeplearning.ai/short-courses/chatgpt-prompt-engineering-for-developers/) — DeepLearning.AI; 1 hr 40 min; Beginner · Paid certificate.
    A 1 hour 40 minute course on writing clear instructions, summarizing, inferring, transforming, and expanding text with an LLM API. Basic Python is helpful, but the prompting principles apply far beyond code.
  - [MCP: Build Rich-Context AI Apps with Anthropic](https://www.deeplearning.ai/short-courses/mcp-build-rich-context-ai-apps-with-anthropic/) — DeepLearning.AI; ~2 hours; Intermediate · Paid certificate.
    A focused two-hour short course on building applications with the Model Context Protocol, made with Anthropic. You should be comfortable with Python and basic LLM concepts. Lessons are free.
4. **Build your first agent loop.** Learn the goal → tool → observation → stop loop. Take Hugging Face's Agents course for structured practice, with Microsoft's AI Agents curriculum as a second perspective.
  - [Hugging Face AI Agents Course](https://huggingface.co/learn/agents-course) — Hugging Face; 3–4 hours per unit; Intermediate · Free certificate.
    A free course from beginner to agent builder. Learn tool use, agent loops, smolagents, LlamaIndex, and LangGraph, then build and share an agent. Basic Python is expected; two free certificate levels are available.
  - [AI Agents for Beginners](https://github.com/microsoft/ai-agents-for-beginners) — Microsoft; 18 lessons (self-paced); Intermediate.
    Eighteen open lessons on agent design patterns, retrieval, tool use, multi-agent systems, and responsible development, with runnable samples. Basic coding helps, and there is no certificate or paywall.
5. **Connect tools safely with MCP.** Build an MCP mental model with the Hugging Face course, then use Microsoft's curriculum and the official docs when you need implementation detail. Start read-only, with explicit permissions.
  - [Hugging Face MCP Course](https://huggingface.co/learn/mcp-course) — Hugging Face; 3–4 hours per unit; Intermediate · Free certificate.
    A free course built with Anthropic that explains the Model Context Protocol and walks you through building MCP applications. You should know basic AI, APIs, and either Python or TypeScript. Free certificates are available.
  - [MCP for Beginners](https://github.com/microsoft/mcp-for-beginners) — Microsoft; 15+ modules (self-paced); Intermediate.
    A free, open curriculum on Model Context Protocol fundamentals, security, and practical labs, with examples across multiple languages. Useful as a reference shelf while you build your first MCP server.
  - [Model Context Protocol Documentation](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/) — Model Context Protocol; Reference; Advanced.
    The official open-standard documentation for MCP: architecture, servers, clients, tools, resources, and prompts. Use it as your reference when a course moves too fast, or when you are designing your own tool connection.
6. **Ship a small capstone.** Use the Kaggle/Google agent intensive for structure and community, or design your own project. The finish line is a tested tool with a README—not a folder of half-finished demos.
  - [5-Day AI Agents Intensive Course with Google](https://www.kaggle.com/learn-guide/5-day-agents) — Kaggle Learn; 5 days + project; Intermediate.
    A self-paced, hands-on agent program from Kaggle and Google: daily codelabs, whitepapers, a capstone project, and community discussion. It is free to follow; cohort-specific badges or certificates can change, so check the current guide.

### The finish line: A small tool with a tool-use boundary

Build one narrow agent or MCP-connected app: a reading-log helper, a public-data lookup, or a local document Q&A tool. Use synthetic or public data, require confirmation before any write action, and document what it cannot do.

**Proof of work:** A public repo or local project with setup steps, a demo, a privacy note, and evidence that it runs from a clean start.

**Then what?** Go deeper into evaluation, observability, and security. Or use the finished project as the foundation for an ASDALE DYNAMICS template or small product.

## 04. Free AI coding and local models

**Beginner to intermediate · 6–8 weeks · 4–6 hours per week**

> A practical toolkit for GitHub, an AI coding partner, and private models that run on your own machine.

Set up GitHub, learn to use Copilot as a careful pair, run an open model locally with Ollama, connect a tool through MCP, and finish one small local-first project you can explain.

- **Best for:** Writers, small-business owners, and early builders who want a public code workspace, an AI coding assistant, and a model that runs locally.
- **Prerequisites:** Comfort with a browser and basic file navigation. No prior programming is required for the GitHub step; later steps assume a little Python or willingness to follow setup instructions.
- **Certificates:** Hugging Face's MCP certificate is free. GitHub's optional certification exam is paid; Copilot and Ollama do not issue certificates.

### The route

1. **Make GitHub your workshop.** Work through GitHub Foundations Part 1. Learn repositories, commits, branches, pull requests, Projects, and Markdown. Publish a small README as your first artifact—not a perfect portfolio.
  - [GitHub Foundations Part 1 of 2](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/training/paths/github-foundations/) — Microsoft Learn; 8 modules (self-paced); Beginner · Paid certificate.
    A free, eight-module learning path covering Git and GitHub essentials: repositories, commits, branches, pull requests, Projects, Markdown, Codespaces, code scanning, and an introduction to GitHub Copilot. The certification exam is separate.
2. **Pair with an AI coding assistant.** Use GitHub Copilot Fundamentals to learn what the assistant is good at, where it is confidently wrong, and how prompt context changes the result. Keep reviewing every suggestion before accepting it.
  - [GitHub Copilot Fundamentals Part 1 of 2](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/training/paths/copilot/) — Microsoft Learn; 9 modules (self-paced); Beginner.
    A free, nine-module path on using GitHub Copilot responsibly: plan choices, prompt engineering, Copilot Spaces, advanced features, IDE and command-line use, developer workflows, and unit testing with an AI assistant.
  - [Kaggle: Python](https://www.kaggle.com/learn/python) — Kaggle Learn; 5 hours; Beginner · Free certificate.
    Five hours of hands-on Python in the browser: variables, functions, loops, lists, dictionaries, and importing libraries. Short coding exercises and a free completion certificate make it a low-friction first coding step.
3. **Run a model on your own machine.** Follow Ollama's official quickstart: install it, pull a small open model, and make one local request. Test a task with fabricated or public data, and note the memory and speed limits you observe.
  - [Ollama Quickstart](https://docs.ollama.com/quickstart) — Ollama; ~1 hour; Beginner.
    An official quickstart for running open models locally: install Ollama, pull a model, connect a desktop app or coding agent, and make a first local API request without sending prompts to a hosted service.
4. **Connect a tool with MCP.** Learn the MCP mental model with Hugging Face's free course, then use Microsoft's curriculum for implementation detail. Start read-only and give the tool the narrowest permissions that still work.
  - [Hugging Face MCP Course](https://huggingface.co/learn/mcp-course) — Hugging Face; 3–4 hours per unit; Intermediate · Free certificate.
    A free course built with Anthropic that explains the Model Context Protocol and walks you through building MCP applications. You should know basic AI, APIs, and either Python or TypeScript. Free certificates are available.
  - [MCP for Beginners](https://github.com/microsoft/mcp-for-beginners) — Microsoft; 15+ modules (self-paced); Intermediate.
    A free, open curriculum on Model Context Protocol fundamentals, security, and practical labs, with examples across multiple languages. Useful as a reference shelf while you build your first MCP server.
  - [Model Context Protocol Documentation](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/) — Model Context Protocol; Reference; Advanced.
    The official open-standard documentation for MCP: architecture, servers, clients, tools, resources, and prompts. Use it as your reference when a course moves too fast, or when you are designing your own tool connection.
5. **Ship a local-first capstone.** Use Microsoft's Generative AI curriculum and Hugging Face's Agents course as references, then build one narrow helper. Keep prompts and files on your machine, require confirmation before writes, and document what the tool cannot do.
  - [Generative AI for Beginners](https://github.com/microsoft/generative-ai-for-beginners) — Microsoft; 21 lessons (self-paced); Beginner.
    Twenty-one lessons that move from prompt basics to retrieval-augmented generation, agents, and responsible use, with Python code samples. You can read the ideas without using Azure, though a little coding helps.
  - [Hugging Face AI Agents Course](https://huggingface.co/learn/agents-course) — Hugging Face; 3–4 hours per unit; Intermediate · Free certificate.
    A free course from beginner to agent builder. Learn tool use, agent loops, smolagents, LlamaIndex, and LangGraph, then build and share an agent. Basic Python is expected; two free certificate levels are available.

### The finish line: A private local-first helper

Build a small note organizer, document Q&A tool, or reading-log helper that runs against a local Ollama model. Use synthetic or public text, keep secrets out of the repo, and add one read-only MCP tool if the project calls for it.

**Proof of work:** A GitHub repository with setup steps, a short demo, a privacy note, two failure cases, and evidence that it runs from a clean start without paid API keys.

**Then what?** Add tests and an evaluation checklist, or turn the project into a reusable template. If you want deeper model work, move into Python and data or the deep-learning route.

## 05. Deep learning, from first principles

**Advanced · 6–9 months · 6–10 hours per week**

> A slower, more rigorous route for people who want to understand the machinery, not only use the API.

Use code-first courses to keep momentum, open textbooks to fill the math, and primary lectures to see how the field reasons. Expect to loop back, not master everything in one pass.

- **Best for:** Builders who have finished a first ML project and now want the math, model internals, and research habits behind modern deep learning.
- **Prerequisites:** Comfortable Python, basic calculus and linear algebra, and at least one machine-learning course or equivalent project experience.
- **Certificates:** Kaggle's Intro to Deep Learning certificate is free. The most valuable output here is a reproducible project and model card.

### The route

1. **Refresh the math you need.** Use selected chapters of Mathematics for Machine Learning and visual videos. Aim for intuition about vectors, gradients, and probability—not exam perfection.
  - [Mathematics for Machine Learning](https://mml-book.github.io/) — MML Book; Reference; Advanced.
    A free textbook that fills the math gaps behind machine learning: linear algebra, analytic geometry, probability, optimization, and modeling. Use selected chapters as a companion, not a prerequisite gate.
  - [3Blue1Brown](https://www.youtube.com/@3blue1brown) — 3Blue1Brown; Watch 2–3 videos; All levels.
    Visual, carefully paced explanations of neural networks, calculus, and linear algebra. Start with the neural-network series when jargon starts to blur; the animations build intuition faster than definitions alone.
2. **Build first.** Take fast.ai's code-first course alongside its free book. Train models, break things, and develop a feel for learning rates, overfitting, and data quality before diving into proofs.
  - [Practical Deep Learning for Coders](https://course.fast.ai/) — fast.ai; 9 lessons (~90 min each) + practice; Intermediate.
    Jeremy Howard's nine-lesson, code-first course takes you from training a first model to deploying practical deep-learning projects. It is free and famously approachable, but it is not a first coding course.
  - [Deep Learning for Coders with fastai and PyTorch](https://fastai.github.io/fastbook2e/) — fast.ai; 20+ hours; Intermediate.
    The free online edition of Jeremy Howard and Sylvain Gugger's hands-on deep-learning book. It is the companion to the fast.ai course and assumes you can already code.
3. **Connect theory to implementation.** Read and run Dive into Deep Learning, then complete Kaggle's short deep-learning course as a checkpoint. Rewrite at least one model from scratch in a small notebook.
  - [Dive into Deep Learning](https://d2l.ai/) — D2L.ai; Semester-scale; Advanced.
    An open, interactive deep-learning textbook that combines math, prose, and runnable code. Adopted by hundreds of universities; free online, with a print edition if you prefer paper.
  - [Kaggle: Intro to Deep Learning](https://www.kaggle.com/learn/intro-to-deep-learning) — Kaggle Learn; 4 hours; Intermediate · Free certificate.
    Use TensorFlow and Keras to build a first neural network, add layers, train with stochastic gradient descent, and recognize overfitting. Four focused hours with a free certificate attached.
4. **Learn from primary lectures.** Watch MIT 6.S191 and selected Andrej Karpathy lectures. Take notes as if you will explain each idea to a smart colleague who has never trained a model.
  - [MIT Introduction to Deep Learning (6.S191)](https://introtodeeplearning.com/) — MIT; Semester lectures + labs; Advanced.
    MIT's fast-paced introduction to deep learning: nine lectures and three software labs spanning vision, sequence models, generative AI, and more. The site labels it beginner-friendly, but calculus and linear algebra help.
  - [Andrej Karpathy](https://www.youtube.com/@AndrejKarpathy) — Andrej Karpathy; Watch one lecture; Advanced.
    Deep, code-first explanations of language models, backpropagation, and building neural networks from scratch. Best when you have some Python and want to understand what is happening under the hood.
5. **Read the reference shelf.** Use Deep Learning as a reference when a concept will not click, and An Introduction to Statistical Learning to keep classical machine learning in view. You are not required to read every page.
  - [Deep Learning](https://www.deeplearningbook.org/) — MIT Press; Reference; Advanced.
    The canonical deep-learning reference by Goodfellow, Bengio, and Courville, free to read in HTML. It is rigorous and dense—a reference to grow into, not a first book for most beginners.
  - [An Introduction to Statistical Learning](https://www.statlearning.com/) — StatLearning; Long reference; Intermediate.
    A respected, approachable textbook on statistical learning, with free PDFs and companion labs in R and Python. It is a patient bridge from classical statistics to modern machine learning.
6. **Fine-tune and document.** Use the Hugging Face LLM course for a guided fine-tuning or evaluation project, then write a model card with intended use, limits, data provenance, and failure cases.
  - [Hugging Face LLM Course](https://huggingface.co/learn/llm-course) — Hugging Face; 6–8 hours per chapter; Intermediate.
    A free, twelve-chapter course on transformers, datasets, tokenizers, fine-tuning, and modern LLM techniques using the Hugging Face ecosystem. It expects good Python and benefits from an intro deep-learning course.

### The finish line: A reproducible model study

Choose a public dataset or small open model. Establish a baseline, test one clear hypothesis, document the compute budget, evaluate beyond a single score, and state who should not rely on it.

**Proof of work:** A reproducible repository, training log, evaluation table, and a model card written for a non-expert reader.

**Then what?** Specialize in evaluation, interpretability, efficient fine-tuning, or safety—and keep a small project shipping while you read.
