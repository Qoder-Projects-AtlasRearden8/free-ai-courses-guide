Why this exists

The free internet is full of AI advice. Much of it is trying to sell you something.

“Learn AI for free” usually means one of three things: a genuinely free course, a free first lesson attached to a paid program, or a free video that ends in a pitch. None of those are automatically bad. The problem is when the labels blur.

This guide is the shelf I wish existed when the AI conversation started moving faster than the vocabulary: 59 resources from providers with a reputation to protect, each linked at the source, each labeled for level, time, and certificate reality.

It is built for the person learning after a shift, between client calls, or after the kids are asleep. The smallest useful step counts.

Who made it

How resources get in

The editorial rules.

A resource is included only when it clears all five bars. Popularity is not one of them.

  1. 01

    Official door

    The link goes to the provider’s own domain—Google, Microsoft, AWS, DeepLearning.AI, Hugging Face, Coursera, edX, Kaggle, freeCodeCamp, fast.ai, or another named publisher.

  2. 02

    Substantive learning

    Not a lead magnet. The resource teaches something useful, and its description says what you will actually do.

  3. 03

    Honest access

    Free-to-learn, preview, paid certificate, and no certificate are separate labels. “Free” never quietly means “free trial.”

  4. 04

    No monetized detour

    No affiliate links, tracking parameters, resale listings, or mystery course mills. If a provider charges, the link goes to the provider, not a middleman.

  5. 05

    Checked by hand

    Every link was checked against the official page on October 23, 2026. The guide keeps that verification date visible because free access changes.

What you won’t find

A quiet website, on purpose.

No analytics, advertising, affiliate links, account system, remote fonts, or third-party scripts. The site does not need to know who you are to teach you something.

  • No promises of a job. Learning is real; hiring is a separate, human process.
  • No “certified expert” inflation. We say completion certificate when that is what it is.
  • No medical advice. Healthcare examples are education only, and never use real patient data.
  • No purchase before understanding. Start with free learning. Pay only when you know what you are buying.

Local-first by design

Your progress is yours.

Saved resources, done marks, and 90-day plan checkboxes are stored in your browser’s local storage. They are not sent to a server. There is no account to create and nothing to log in to.

Clear your browser’s site data and the marks disappear. Use the same browser and the same site address to keep them. This is the honest trade-off of a local-first tool.

What is stored

  • Resource IDs you save or mark done.
  • Plan task IDs you check.

What is not stored

  • Your name, email, search terms, or browsing history.
  • Any prompt, note, or project content.
  • Any patient, client, financial, or password information.

A necessary boundary

Healthcare workers are welcome. Patient data is not.

This guide is written with nurses and other healthcare workers in mind because that is part of the author’s background. The healthcare-related examples are education only. They are not medical advice, clinical guidance, a diagnostic tool, or a substitute for your professional standards and employer policy.

Never put real patient information, identifiers, images, or anything covered by professional confidentiality into a consumer AI tool. Practice with fabricated scenarios. Check your employer’s rules and your professional obligations before using AI in any work context.

A seat for everyone

Plain language is an accessibility tool.

The guide uses real headings, keyboard-friendly controls, visible focus states, strong text contrast, reduced-motion support, and print styles for the study plan. It does not use autoplay, pop-ups, or fake urgency. If a provider’s course is inaccessible to you, that is worth knowing before you invest your time; contact the provider about its accommodations.

No account. No pitch.

Pick up one good habit.

Learn a little, make something small, and check the output. That habit is worth more than a shelf of certificates.
