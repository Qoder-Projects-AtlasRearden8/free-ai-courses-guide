The 90-day plan

The 90-day plain-English AI plan

A humane learning plan built around short sessions, small artifacts, and real life.

Built for low-energy weeks

Standard pace is about 4–5 hours per week. On a hard week, do the first task only. On a good week, add the stretch. Progress is not a personality test.

Your checked tasks are stored only in this browser.

This plan assumes you have a job, a family, or a questionable relationship with your calendar. Three focused sessions a week are enough. Every week has a small finish line, every phase has a checkpoint, and two flex days are built into each 30-day stretch. You do not need to finish every resource—the plan is the spine, and the library is the shelf you choose from.

Day 90 checkpoint: you made a thing

The finish line is not a certificate. It is a working artifact, a readable explanation, and a realistic next step.

  • A tested tool, notebook, or manual with a README anyone can follow.
  • One prompt library or workflow you actually reuse.
  • A personal privacy and safety card for AI use.
  • A short case study that names limitations instead of hiding them.
  • A decision about the next 90 days—not a vague intention to 'learn more'.

The house rules

Keep the plan humane.

A schedule is a tool for making room, not another place to fail. These rules matter more than finishing every checkbox.

  1. The plan is the spine; the library is the shelf. You do not need to finish every course.
  2. Three short sessions a week count. Missing a week is data, not a moral failure.
  3. Always protect privacy first: no patient information, client records, passwords, API keys, or private financial data in prompts or repositories.
  4. Use AI for drafts, questions, and scaffolding. Keep human judgment for decisions, claims, and anything high-stakes.
  5. When a resource stops helping, stop. Provider loyalty is not a learning outcome.
  6. If you are exhausted, do the ten-minute version. Rest is part of the schedule.

Real life happens

When the plan meets a hard week.

No guilt, no dramatic restart. Use the smallest response that keeps the thread.

You missed a week

Restart at the current week. Do not restart from Week 1. Skip the stretch task and keep the next checkpoint small.

You only have 15 minutes

Open the next task, set a timer, and do one small piece. Write one sentence in your learning note before you close it.

A course format is not working

Substitute a video or a different route from the library, but keep the week's artifact. The artifact is the real test.

Life is genuinely on fire

Pause the plan. Keep your notes. When you return, resume at the current week and shrink the next checkpoint. Do not punish yourself with a restart.

Perfectionism shows up

Ship the boring first version. A small, documented thing that works teaches more than a beautiful plan that never runs.

Practice with a point

Three projects worth making.

A project turns course language into your own judgment. Keep it small, document the limits, and use fabricated or public information only.

For authors and small businesses

Build a prompt library for book descriptions, FAQ drafts, and email replies—using only public information. Add a human review step and a fact-check checklist.

For healthcare workers, education only

Practice with fabricated scenarios. Create a study guide or shift-reflection template, never a diagnostic or treatment tool, and never paste real patient information into a consumer AI tool.

For local-first tinkerers

Compare a hosted model with a small local model on one low-stakes task. Record privacy, cost, speed, and quality trade-offs in a one-page note.

Healthcare examples in this guide are educational. They are not diagnostic, treatment, or clinical decision tools, and real patient information never belongs in a consumer AI prompt.

Day 90

The finish line is a thing you made.

You do not need a certificate to prove that. A small working artifact, a clear write-up, and a realistic next step are stronger evidence than a badge alone.

  • One tested artifact with a README and a clear limitation.
  • One reusable prompt or workflow, stored somewhere you will find it again.
  • One personal safety and privacy card.
  • One honest explanation of what a certificate can and cannot prove.
  • One concrete next step for the following 90 days.
